# Types of Cables we're going to encounter 

In Cisco Packet Tracer, we have these available cables from right to left 
<img width="542" height="84" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/6919b008-3990-4786-b4c6-c6c6395e99b6" />

1. **Console**
- Used to access across a device's CLI for initial configuration and for configuration, it doesn't carry traffic
2. **Copper Straigh-Through**
- The standard ethernet cable used to connect different types of devices
3. **Copper Crossover**
- Ethernet cable that was traditionally used to connect similar devices
4. **Single Mode Fiber**
- Fiber-optic cable designed for long distance connections
5. **Multi Mode Fiber**
- Fiber-optic cable used for short distances and high-speed connections 
6. **Phone**
- Telephone related interfaces and devices
7. **Coaxial**
- Used in cable broadband and older coaxial networking technologies
8. **Serial DCE**
- Serial WAN cable endpoint that provides clocking for the connection
9. **Serial DTE**
- Serial WAN cable endpoint that receives clocking from the DCE side 
10. **Octal**
- Connects multiple ports to a terminal/access server for device management
11. **IoT Custom Cable**
- For IoT compatible components 
12. **USB Cable**
- Connects devices through compatible USB interfaces

Most used: 
1. **Copper-Straight through**, since it's for most basic Ethernet connections, like PC to switch and router to switch.
2. **Copper crossover**, for similar devices like switch to switch or PC to PC connections
3. **Console cable/Rollover Cable**, for configuring routers and switches directly through their console ports
- Usually now, it's an USB to RJ-45
